CARER AT-RISK MAP — WHAT DATA WE HAVE AND WHAT WE CAN PLOT
Innovate for Ireland · AI Build Day
Last updated: 8 June 2026

A plain-language summary for the team, so we can decide together what
to actually put on the map.


============================================================
THE IDEA
============================================================
How might we help a carer-support service see where unpaid carers
across Ireland are most at risk of burnout, and where that need will
grow as the population ages, so support reaches them before crisis?

Decisions so far:
  - For whom:        a carer-support service
  - Where:           Ireland, national
  - Map:             8 regions (NUTS3), with zoom-in to small areas
  - Building first:  the current map of where carers are most in need


============================================================
WHAT "AT-RISK" MEANS HERE
============================================================
We can't see individual carers in open data, so we map where, by
area, the things that drive burnout pile up:

  - heavy care load    (lots of carers; many caring 43+ hrs/week)
  - vulnerable carers  (older, living alone, in deprived areas)
  - rising demand      (an ageing population needing more care)


============================================================
WHAT WE CAN PLOT   (all free, open CSO data, no logins or keys)
============================================================
Everything below comes from Census 2022 and is published at every
map level we need: 8 regions right down to small areas (~18,000),
so we can show the whole country and zoom in.

1. THE CORE: where carers are   (ready to build now)
     - Carer rate: carers as a share of the local population.
       Answers "where is caring most common?"

2. HOW HARD THE CARING IS   (risk intensity)
     - Share caring 43+ hours/week  (heaviest burden; ~29% nationally)
     - Older carers, 65+            (caring when you may need care too)
     - Lone carers                  (caring while living alone)

3. WHAT MAKES AN AREA RISKIER   (context)
     - Deprivation (Pobal index): less able to afford private respite
     - Ageing population, now and projected to 2042: rising demand
     - Isolation: distance to the 22 Family Carers Ireland centres,
       and low car ownership


============================================================
ABOUT "65+ NOW AND PROJECTED TO 2042"
============================================================
This is the DEMAND side: the people who need care, not the carers.

  - "65+ share now" = how much of an area is aged 65+ today. More
    older people means more people likely to need care, which means
    more load on the local carers.

  - "projected to 2042" = the CSO publishes regional population
    projections showing how the 65+ share grows over the next ~20
    years. It rises everywhere, but faster in some regions.

So this layer lets the map say not just "demand is high here now"
but "demand here will grow fastest, plan for it." That is the
get-ahead-of-the-crisis story for a support service.

(Note: 65+ shows up twice on purpose. As people being CARED FOR it
is demand, which is this section. As CARERS who are themselves
elderly it is a vulnerability, listed under "how hard the caring is.")


============================================================
THE DECISION FOR THE TEAM
============================================================
The carer-rate base map is the same whatever we choose, so it is
being built now. The open question is how we show "at-risk" on top:

  A. Separate layers you toggle on and off  (simplest, most honest)
  B. A two-variable map, e.g. carer rate x deprivation
  C. One combined "at-risk" score


============================================================
THE FINE PRINT   (worth knowing, not blockers)
============================================================
  - We map area rates, not individuals. Open data can't tell us
    which specific carers are poor or unwell; we show where these
    things overlap by area.
  - Future projections exist only at region level (8 regions), not
    small area. So the "future" view is coarser than the "now" view.
  - We avoid the "+50% since 2016" headline: the census question
    changed wording, so it is not a clean comparison.


============================================================
TECHNICAL REFERENCE   (for whoever builds it)
============================================================
Source: CSO PxStat API, JSON-stat format, no key required.
  https://ws.cso.ie/public/api.restful/PxStat.Data.Cube_API.ReadDataset/<CODE>/JSON-stat/2.0/en

Key Census 2022 tables:
  Carers by region (NUTS3) ......  SAP2022T12T2NUTS
  Population by region (NUTS3) ...  SAP2022T1T1NUTS
  Carers by small area ..........  SAP2022T12T2SA
  Carer % of population, county .  FY088
  Carers by county ..............  F3064 / F4015 / F4068
  Carers by HSE network .........  DHGA09CHN / CST / HR

Boundaries: use current NUTS3 (2016/2021), NOT the 2015 set
(Louth and Tipperary changed regions between the two).
Projections: CSO Regional Population Projections 2023-2042.
Deprivation: Pobal HP Deprivation Index (separate source, also free).
